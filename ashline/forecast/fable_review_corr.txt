Read all three modules (corr_mixes.py, paired.py, mixes.py) plus the policy and rule helpers. Answers:

**1. Season‑i correlation across mixes is exact, not approximate.** In `mixes.run` the outer `rng` is consumed identically regardless of mix or rule: shuffle of the two non‑focal seats, one uniform per seat, one `random()` per seat per tick, one `getrandbits(32)`. None of those calls depend on `mix` or on the `A.judge`/`A.resolve` variant. Everything mix‑ or rule‑dependent happens inside `play`, which uses its own `random.Random(seed)` reseeded per season from that `getrandbits`. Policies do consume the inner stream differently, so tie‑breaks diverge within a season, but that never feeds back to the outer stream. House moves use sha256 ties and `S.setup` is deterministic, so there is no hidden global `random` use either. Season i therefore shares focal seat, seat order, attendance vectors, and inner seed across all 12 cells of a block.
   How to check: wrap `rng.getrandbits` the same way `paired.per_season` wraps `np.mean`, record the 200 per‑season seeds for each of the 12 cells, and assert the lists are identical. A weaker check is that the base/border2 pairing already relies on this and gives SEs around 0.4 instead of the unpaired 0.6‑ish.

**2. The GLS algebra and `chi2_sf` are correct.** Combining two independent blocks as V = (W₀+W₁)⁻¹, m = V(W₀m₀+W₁m₁), then μ = 1ᵀWm / 1ᵀW1, se = (1ᵀW1)^‑½, Q = (m−μ)ᵀW(m−μ) with df = 6−1 = 5 is the right statistic. `chi2_sf` implements the standard series for the regularized lower incomplete gamma, P(a,x) = e^{−x} x^a /Γ(a) · Σ xⁿ/(a(a+1)…(a+n)), terms are positive and eventually shrink, so it terminates, and 1−P is only inaccurate near P≈1 at the 1e‑12 level. No bug. Two caveats on interpretation: GLS weights can go negative when cells are positively correlated, so μ may not be a convex combination of the six deltas, and Q(df=5) says nothing about the block differences you already see (hh 1.90 vs 1.40, cc 1.25 vs 0.17); a block test (m₀−m₁)ᵀ(C₀+C₁)⁻¹(m₀−m₁) ~ χ²(6) is cheap and worth printing.

**3. The inverse is numerically fine but statistically unnecessary.** A 6×6 correlation matrix from n=200 has entrywise error about 0.07, and unless two mixes are nearly collinear the condition number will be modest, so `inv` is stable. The statistical cost is also small: with estimated covariance Q is a Hotelling T², and the exact reference F(5, n−5) scaled by (n−1)·5/(n−5) differs from χ²(5) by about 3 percent at n=200. The real objection is that you invert two noisy covariances and then invert their sum, for no gain over an equal‑weight estimator at this n.

**The one change:** stack the two blocks into one 400×6 matrix of per‑season vectors (blocks are independent draws of the same process, so this is legitimate) and compute everything from per‑season quantities. Then μ is the mean of the per‑season across‑mix average with SE from those 400 scalars, no inverse at all, and heterogeneity is Hotelling's T² on the five contrasts d_mix − d_hh with F(5, 395) as reference. That replaces the two‑block GLS merge, removes the negative‑weight problem, and uses the exact finite‑sample distribution.
